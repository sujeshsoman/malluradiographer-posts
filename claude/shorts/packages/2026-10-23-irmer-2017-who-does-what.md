**Fact-check list**

- [x] The employer must put written procedures in place (referral guidelines, quality assurance, diagnostic reference levels) and ensure practitioners and operators undertake continuing training; employer duties cannot be delegated. (REF_2 (regulation 6); REF_1 (employer section))
- [x] A referrer is a registered healthcare professional who refers individuals to a practitioner and must supply sufficient medical data; referrers must follow the employer's procedures. (REF_2 (regulations 2, 10); REF_1)
- [x] A practitioner is a registered healthcare professional entitled by the employer to take responsibility for justifying an individual exposure; justification weighs benefit, detriment and alternatives with less or no radiation. (REF_2 (regulations 2, 10, 11); REF_1)
- [x] Authorisation is separate from justification, shows justification has been carried out (for example a signature on a request card) and must happen before the exposure. (REF_1 (authorisation); REF_2 (regulation 11))
- [x] An operator is entitled by the employer to carry out a practical aspect (including performing the exposure, image processing and archiving) and is responsible for each practical aspect carried out; practitioner and operator must keep doses ALARP, and exposures without a reference level must still be optimised. (REF_1 (practical aspect, operator, DRLs); REF_2 (regulations 10, 12))
- [x] One person can act as more than one duty holder, with each entitlement defined in the employer's written procedures. (REF_1 (regulation 2(2), schedule 2))

REF_1: [Guidance to the Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017 (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ionising-radiation-medical-exposure-regulations-2017-guidance/guidance-to-the-ionising-radiation-medical-exposure-regulations-2017) · REF_2: [The Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017 (legislation.gov.uk)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2017/1322/data.htm)

**TTS copy** (same words, same order; only terms a voice might misread are respelled)

You press the exposure button. So did you justify the scan? Not necessarily. Let's sort out who does what. Under I R M E R 2017, the Ionising Radiation Medical Exposure Regulations, responsibility is shared between duty holders. Know them, and exam and interview questions become easy. They also protect your patient. First, the employer. That's the business running the service, not an employee on shift. The employer must put written procedures in place, including referral guidelines, quality assurance and diagnostic reference levels. The employer must also make sure practitioners and operators keep up their training. These legal duties cannot be delegated. Second, the referrer. A registered healthcare professional who refers someone for an exposure. They must give the practitioner enough medical data, like previous diagnostic information, to judge the benefit. Referrers must follow the employer's procedures too. Third, the practitioner. Also a registered healthcare professional, entitled by the employer to take responsibility for justifying an individual exposure. They weigh the benefit against the individual's detriment, and consider alternatives with less or no radiation. Think of a flight. The referrer asks for a seat. The practitioner clears the flight as worthwhile. Authorisation is the signed clearance, such as a signature on a request card. It comes before take-off. Fourth, the operator. That's anyone the employer entitles to carry out a practical aspect of an exposure. That includes performing the exposure, and also image processing and archiving. Operators are responsible for each practical aspect they carry out. Both practitioner and operator must keep doses as low as reasonably practicable. The operator selects equipment and methods, with regard to quality control, patient dose assessment and the employer's diagnostic reference levels. Where no reference level exists, the exposure must still be optimised. Here's the exam trap. Justification is the practitioner's job. Practical aspects are the operator's. One person can hold more than one role, but only if the employer entitles them, in writing, in the employer's procedures. Recap. The employer sets the procedures. The referrer asks. The practitioner justifies. The operator carries out the practical work. And everyone must follow the employer's procedures. Always follow your local protocol. Your turn. A referral arrives for a chest X-ray. In what order do the referrer, practitioner and operator act? Answer in the comments.
